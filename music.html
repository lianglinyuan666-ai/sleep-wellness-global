The Science

How Sound Shapes Your Sleep

🧠 Binaural Beats: Brainwave Entrainment

When each ear receives a slightly different frequency (e.g. 200Hz left, 206Hz right), the brain perceives a 6Hz "beat" — a theta frequency associated with drowsiness. This frequency-following response (FFR) has been documented in EEG studies. Source: IMA Good Sleep · Confidence 8/10

🎵 Pink Noise: Enhancing Slow-Wave Sleep

Pink noise decreases by 3dB per octave — matching how the human auditory system processes sound. A 2017 Frontiers in Human Neuroscience study found pink noise during sleep increased slow-wave activity, especially benefiting older adults' memory consolidation. Source: IMA MCBT-I Sleep · Confidence 9/10

🌊 Nature Sounds: Evolutionary Masking

Rain, waves, and wind are "stochastic" — unpredictable enough to mask sudden noises but smooth enough not to startle. These sounds signal "no immediate threat" to the brain's vigilance system. Source: IMA Good Sleep · Confidence 7/10

🧘 4-7-8 Breathing: The Relaxation Response

The 4-7-8 technique activates the parasympathetic nervous system. A meta-analysis confirmed guided breathing significantly reduces sleep onset latency, with effects comparable to low-dose sleep medication — without side effects. Source: IMA MCBT-I Sleep · Confidence 8/10
